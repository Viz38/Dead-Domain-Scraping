# 🧪 Ghost Forensic Archive Network (GFAN)

Ghost v14.0 utilizes a globally distributed network of 31 archival nodes. This multi-archive approach is designed to overcome "Archive Bias" (where Western archives miss Asian/EU content) and "Snapshot Rot" (where IA snapshots are broken but national archives remain intact).

## 🏛 The Core Four (Blitz Layer)
These are the most reliable, high-volume sources used for the initial sweep.

| Source | Strengths | Reasoning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wayback Machine (IA)** | Massive scale (800B+ pages). | The baseline for all web history. If it's on the web, IA probably saw it. |
| **CommonCrawl** | Raw crawl data from a different engine. | Often contains data IA missed due to different crawler politeness rules. |
| **Archive.is (ph/li/vn)** | Human-triggered snapshots. | Bypasses `robots.txt` and captures what people actually found interesting. |
| **URLScan.io** | Real-time DOM snapshots. | Captures transient redirects and recent "Live" states before they die. |

## 📐 Why 31 Sources?
1. **Redundancy**: No single archive is 100% reliable. If IA is down (or has a "Broken" snapshot), Arquivo.pt or Archive.is acts as the failover.
2. **Geographical Parity**: Many European and Asian domains block "The Wayback Machine" via `robots.txt` but allow their local National Archive to crawl them. Ghost exploits this gap.
3. **Crawl Depth**: CommonCrawl and IA use different algorithms. One might find `/blog` while the other only finds `/index`. Ghost merges both for a "Full Content" view.
4. **Historical Persistence**: Smaller archives often preserve "dead" domains longer than IA, which might purge non-relevant data during maintenance.
